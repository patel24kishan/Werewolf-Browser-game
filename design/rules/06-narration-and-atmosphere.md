# 06 · Narration and Atmosphere

At a table, the moderator is the most important person in the game. Their voice,
pacing and drama decide whether the evening is tense and funny or flat. In our game the
app is the moderator, so everything here becomes a requirement for sound, timing,
lighting and text.

## The moderator's craft

- **Build dread.** Create a spine-tingling mood, especially at night.
- **Draw out the reveal.** Hold back the night's victims a moment longer than feels
  comfortable before naming them.
- **Keep the argument alive.** When the debate dies down, stir it up again.
- **Speak freely.** Any call-out lines are only examples. A good moderator improvises.

## Procedure points that protect secrets

| At the table | Why | In the app |
|---|---|---|
| Never let a role's gender or a slip give it away, e.g. "the Witch used his, um, her potion" | Small slips reveal identities | Narration never names who acted, and uses neutral wording |
| Do not turn your voice toward the player you are waking | Others could work out where that role sits | Every phone shows the same night screen and plays the same sounds, whoever is acting |
| When the Seer looks, pretend to turn over every card | Hides which player was checked | Every player's phone shows the same activity during each night turn |
| Walk around the whole table when choosing the lovers | Hides who was tapped | Every player gets the same private "you may have been touched" moment, true or not |
| When the Little Girl is in play, nobody hides their face | Peeking must be possible and catchable | See the peeking mechanic in [07-digital-adaptation.md](07-digital-adaptation.md) |
| The Sheriff vote can wait as long as you like | Players form opinions first | The app opens the Sheriff vote after a set part of the first debate |

## Narration lines

These are our own lines. They set the tone the app's voice and text should hit.

| Moment | Line |
|---|---|
| Night falls | "The lamps go out across the village. Close your eyes… and pray the night is quiet." |
| Seer wakes | "Seer, open your eyes. Whose soul will you read tonight?" |
| Seer sleeps | "Seer, the vision fades. Sleep." |
| Werewolves wake | "Werewolves, wake. Find your pack… and choose your prey." |
| Werewolves sleep | "The hunt is over. Werewolves, return to your beds with blood on your hands." |
| Witch wakes | "Witch, rise. Here is tonight's victim. Will you heal them, poison another, or let fate run its course?" |
| Dawn, one death | "The sun rises. Everyone wakes… except one." |
| Dawn, no death | "The sun rises on a village that, for once, is whole." |
| Dawn, two deaths | "The sun rises on two empty beds." |
| Vote called | "Enough talk. On my word, point to the one you believe is the beast." |
| Tie | "The village cannot agree. Tonight, no one hangs." |

## Running a good session

- **New groups:** start with the basic mix and add special roles gradually.
- **Experienced groups:** use special roles even with few players. It adds intrigue.
- **Play several short games** in a row and change the special roles each time.
- **Small group variant:** at a very small table, the moderator can also play. In the
  app this is the normal case, because the app moderates.

## Advice to players

| Role | Advice |
|---|---|
| Werewolves | Voting against a fellow wolf can clear your own name, if the village notices |
| Seer | Be careful once you've found a wolf. Revealing yourself to expose them can be worth it, but not too early |
| Little Girl | Peek early. If you hesitate, you may die before learning anything |
| Hunter | Stay hidden. Wolves who know you're the Hunter won't give you a target |
| Cupid | If you're one of the lovers, don't pick a loudmouth as your partner |
| Sheriff | Run for it. As a werewolf, don't campaign too hard |
| Witch | Save your potions. You get stronger as the game goes on |
