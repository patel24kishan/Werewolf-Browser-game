# 01 · Overview

## Premise

A small village is hiding werewolves among its people. Every night the werewolves
wake in secret and kill one villager. Every morning the survivors argue about who
among them is a beast, then vote to eliminate one suspect. Nobody's card is shown
until they are out of the game, so every claim, accusation and alibi can be a lie.

## The two sides

| Side | Who is on it | What they want |
|---|---|---|
| **Village** | Simple Villagers and every special role: Seer, Witch, Hunter, Cupid, Little Girl, Thief. The Sheriff is a title given to a villager-side or wolf-side player, not a side of its own | Find and eliminate every werewolf |
| **Werewolves** | 1 to 4 Werewolves, depending on the player count | Outnumber and kill off the village while staying hidden |

A third, temporary side can appear. When Cupid's two lovers are one werewolf and one
villager, those two leave their teams and play for themselves (see below).

## Player count

The original rules need 8 to 18 players, plus one moderator who runs the game and
does not play. The digital version allows **5 to 18 players**, using a reduced card
mix below 8. The app is the moderator, so every person plays.

## How each side wins

- **The village wins** when every werewolf is eliminated and at least one villager is
  still alive.
- **The werewolves win** when the last villager is eliminated and at least one
  werewolf is still alive.
- **Eliminated players still win** if their side wins. Being killed early does not
  take you out of the victory.
- **Mixed lovers** (one werewolf, one villager) win only if they are the last two
  players alive. Every other player, on both teams, is now their opponent.

The game is checked for a winner after every elimination is fully resolved, day or
night.

## Pace

A game is short, usually 15 to 30 minutes. Groups normally play several games in one
sitting and change which special roles are in the deck between games.

## What makes it work

The booklet is blunt about this: **atmosphere is everything**. The moderator's voice,
pauses and drama turn a simple vote into a tense, funny evening. A digital version has
to carry that atmosphere through sound, timing, light and narration, or the game
loses its heart. See [06-narration-and-atmosphere.md](06-narration-and-atmosphere.md).
